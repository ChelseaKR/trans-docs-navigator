## 1. System description and intended purpose

Privacy-first, fully-cited navigator for legal name and gender-marker changes
(CA/IL/NY/TX/WA + federal, EN/ES). Generation is retrieval-mandatory over a
hand-verified corpus; the default generator is a deterministic extractive composer, with
a Bedrock (Claude) seam behind the identical `citation.enforce()` gate. **Information,
not legal advice** — not a filing service; no individualized legal conclusions
(machine-gated disclosure on every page). Deployment status: **demonstration/preview**;
all launch gates OPEN, no corpus record named-human-verified yet.

## 2. Interested parties and how they are affected

| Party | Relationship | Principal impacts (benefit / harm potential) |
|---|---|---|
| Trans and nonbinary individuals (primary users), including people in hostile jurisdictions | Use guidance to change legal documents | **Benefit:** ordered, cited, plain-language checklists; fee-waiver visibility; privacy-by-design. **Harm potential:** acting on wrong/stale guidance (R1); exposure via request/URL/provider records (R2, DPIA §5); inequitable coverage by state/language (R3); mistaking seed content for verified fact (R4) |
| Supporters, community navigators, case workers | Assist primary users | Same guidance benefits/harms at one remove; wrong guidance propagates through trusted relationships |
| Legal-aid organizations and clinics | Prospective distribution channel and verifier pool | Reputational stake in accuracy; benefit from verification workbench/coverage honesty |
| Named verifiers and counsel | Human gate-keepers | Their names attach to records/sign-offs; the system must never simulate or placeholder-sign for them (machine-enforced) |
| Operator / project owner | Runs the service | UPL exposure (R8); duty to honor the stated retention and disclosure posture |
| Society / public discourse | Indirect | Honest, cited guidance displacing forum lore; conversely, any overclaim would damage trust in a domain where trust is scarce |

## 3. Reasonably foreseeable impacts

### 3.1 Intended use
- Correct, cited guidance shortens a hostile, error-prone bureaucratic process.
- Refusal-over-invention: where no current verified record exists, the system says so
  and points at the official source — an honest gap instead of a guess.
- Privacy posture (DPIA §1–3) minimizes what any adversary can obtain.

### 3.2 Misuse and over-reliance
- Treating output as legal advice despite the banner (R8) — mitigated by the
  machine-gated disclosure and the absence of individualized-conclusion templates;
  counsel review of the framing is an OPEN gate.
- An adversary mining request/provider records to identify users (R2/DPIA threat
  model) — mitigated by minimization; residual surfaces are inventoried, not denied.
- Third parties re-serving corpus content without its freshness/citation context —
  relevant to any future public-dataset distribution (roadmap F15; not shipped).

### 3.3 Failure modes
- Hallucinated or stale legal fact (R1, R5): blocked structurally by
  `citation.enforce()` + freshness fail-closed; measured by the eval gates; the
  residual is content-level error inside a verified record — which is why named-human
  verification is a launch gate, not a nicety.
- Corpus tampering between CI and serve: boot-time manifest verification refuses to
  start on mismatch (DPIA §6b).
- Inequitable degradation (R3): a state/language with thin coverage renders honest
  gaps; the failure would be *silent* inequity, which the disaggregated eval and
  coverage matrix exist to surface.

## 4. Severity and likelihood

This assessment deliberately does **not** restate numeric ratings. Likelihood/impact per
risk live in [`residual-risk.md`](./residual-risk.md) (dated, versioned); accuracy and
parity measurements live in [`eval-report.md`](./eval-report.md) (regenerated by
`make eval`). Restating them here would create a second, driftable copy. The dominant
severity fact: the primary user population can face legal, financial, and physical
consequences from both wrong guidance and privacy failure — which is why every mitigation
above is merge-blocking rather than advisory.

## 5. Controls relied upon

Citation enforcement · freshness fail-closed · disclosure gates · privacy/egress gates ·
disaggregated eval thresholds · launch-gate derivation (placeholder verifiers cannot
sign) · corpus integrity attestation · bounded/allowlisted logging. Enforcement mapping:
DPIA §3 and [`ai-risk-register.md`](./ai-risk-register.md).

## 6. Monitoring and re-assessment triggers

Re-open this assessment when any of the following occurs (same cadence discipline as the
DPIA's "per data-flow change"):
- the Bedrock generator is configured in any deployed environment (also requires the
  real-Bedrock eval launch gate and provider-retention review);
- a new jurisdiction, language, or situational content branch ships;
- any new data flow opens (re-opens the DPIA by standing rule);
- a launch gate flips to DONE (the assessment's deployment-status premises change).
