# Synthetic user research panel — Trans Docs Navigator

> Run 2026-06-17. 18 synthetic personas spanning the real user base (covered and uncovered states, name/gender/both, English / Spanish-dominant / Spanish-only, screen-reader and low-literacy users, a minor, undocumented, DV-survivor, reentry, interstate, and a legal-aid caseworker) each attempted their real goal against the live product and scored it 1–5, then a research agent synthesized. Grounded in the deployed preview.

## Verdict

**Meets the mark: NO.**  Average satisfaction **2.67/5**.  Goal achieved: 1 yes · 13 partial · 4 no (of 18).  Frictions: 19 blocker · 36 major · 35 minor.

No — Trans Docs Navigator earns deep trust but doesn't yet deliver on its core promise. It is the most honest, privacy-respecting, emotionally validating doc-change tool the panel has seen, and for legal NAME changes in its 5 covered states it is genuinely excellent. But its actual product pitch — helping a diverse trans population update name AND gender marker — collapses almost everywhere: gender-marker is non-actionable in nearly every state (especially federal SSA/passport), 45 states can't even be selected, and the highest-stakes user situations (undocumented, DV survivor, minor, no fixed address, recently incarcerated, interstate move) are met with silence. The team's suspicion is correct: it's a trustworthy name-change orientation tool wearing the clothes of a comprehensive name-and-gender-marker navigator.

## Top blockers

- Gender-marker changes are non-actionable almost everywhere (federal SSA/passport universally degraded; most states show no actionable marker steps) — half the product's promise is undeliverable today.
- Only 5 states are selectable; the other 45 hit a dead-end intake with no warning and no referral, even though the engine can degrade gracefully by URL.
- High-risk user situations (undocumented, DV survivor/confidential filing, minor, no fixed address, reentry, interstate move) are invisible, and the checklist reads as complete while omitting the one fact that changes everything for those users — which is actively dangerous, not just incomplete.
- Content is unverified seed/demo data with placeholder verifiers, blocking professional recommendation (caseworker) and full reliance on a high-stakes legal task.

## Quick wins (high value, low effort)

- On every 'needs reverification' step, keep the official source link + last-checked date visible and add a one-line 'why' (policy in flux) plus a 'safest thing to do today' pointer to the .gov front door. Closes the 'honest but stranded' gap for ~15 personas at low cost.
- Add an upfront homepage note near the state dropdown: 'We currently cover 5 states. If yours isn't listed, here's where to go,' and make the existing graceful out-of-state degrade path reachable from the intake form, not just by URL editing.
- Link the actual FW-001 / Statement of Inability to Afford Payment fee-waiver forms with one line of plain-language eligibility (income / public-benefits shortcut) on the court-order step and form page.
- Fix the broken routes and standardize URL schemes (/form vs /forms, NC-100 casing, court-order/passport 404s) and the lang=es vs language=es inconsistency so a shared/typed link never silently drops to English.
- Add a 'Get the official form' link to the gating court-order step in every covered state (it already exists for SS-5/DS-82).
- Add a National Center for Transgender Equality / Lambda Legal / state trans-legal-aid directory link wherever the tool says 'talk to a lawyer,' so the dead-end always hands over a real lifeline.
- Confirm X/nonbinary marker availability explicitly per state (e.g. 'Illinois driver's license: X is available') instead of the vague 'self-certify your sex designation.'
- Add a visible 'Copied!' confirmation (already an aria-live region for screen readers — surface it visually) and plain-language paste instructions on the copy helper.
- Surface a worst-case total cost line (e.g. '$435–480 court fee, possibly $0 with a waiver, plus ~$X DMV') instead of 'Estimated cost: varies.'

## Strategic gaps

- Federal gender-marker volatility has no product strategy. Because SSA/passport are degraded everywhere, the marker half of the product is non-functional nationally. The team needs a stance: a maintained 'current federal status' explainer with official links and dates, not per-step silence.
- No coverage model for the 45 uncovered states — either expand coverage or commit to a strong, referral-rich out-of-state experience as a first-class path.
- No handling for the high-risk personas who most need help and are most likely to be harmed by a 'complete-looking' but incomplete checklist: confidential/sealed filings and publication waivers for DV survivors, immigration-exposure warnings for undocumented users, minor/parental-consent flows, no-stable-address guidance, reentry considerations, and interstate-move/recognition questions.
- No human/legal verification layer. Without a 'last verified by [named person/org]' attestation, legal aid cannot recommend it and high-stakes users won't rely on it — this caps the product's authority regardless of UX polish.
- The grounded Q&A can't take the user's real situational question (survivor confidentiality, undocumented safety, minor status), so it gives false confidence that the generic process IS the whole picture.
- Accessibility is structurally good but unverified by a human screen-reader pass before launch — shipping unaudited to blind users on a legal task is a trust and liability gap.
- Plain-language / literacy tier is missing — the product targets a population with wide literacy and digital-literacy ranges but writes at ~8th–10th grade with legalese; no read-aloud, icons, or simplified mode.
- Scope creep gaps in the 'whole transition' promise: financial/other-records updates (bank, employer, insurance, voter reg) and divorce-based former-name restoration are collected or implied but unsupported.

## Prioritized recommendations

- **P0 — Make every degraded ('needs reverification') step actionable-by-redirect: always show the official .gov source link + last-checked date, a one-line 'why this is unverified' (federal policy in flux), and a 'safest action today' pointer. Never strip the link on degraded steps.**  
  This is the single highest-leverage fix: ~15 of 18 personas praise the honesty but are stranded by it. It converts the product's biggest emotional asset (candor) into actual utility with minimal engineering, and directly addresses the federal gender-marker dead end that breaks the product's core promise.
- **P0 — Ship a referral + safety-warning layer: named, vetted trans/legal-aid orgs per region (with Spanish-speaking options), plus explicit pre-filing warnings before any step that carries real risk — public/published court petition, federal-agency contact for undocumented users, and the existence of confidential/sealed filing and publication waivers.**  
  The most vulnerable personas (P5 undocumented, P15 DV survivor, P13 reentry, P6/P4 unsupported states) are not just unserved — they could be harmed by following a checklist that reads as complete. P15's 'it protects my browser data; it ignores my life' is the sharpest indictment in the panel. Warnings + referrals are far cheaper than full case coverage and remove the danger.
- **P0 — Establish a human/legal verification process and surface a 'last verified by [named person/org]' stamp; remove placeholder verifiers before any wider promotion.**  
  Unverified seed data is the ceiling on the product's authority. The caseworker (P14) — the highest-leverage distribution channel to the entire user base — explicitly cannot recommend it without this, and high-stakes individual users (P9, P11) won't rely on it. No UX polish overcomes 'nobody's name is on this.'
- **P1 — Fix the front door for uncovered states: add an upfront coverage note by the dropdown, allow any state to be selected so the graceful out-of-state path (federal steps + honest 'not yet covered' + named legal-aid) is reachable from intake, not just by URL.**  
  45 states currently fail at step one with no warning (P4, P6), and the engine already supports graceful degradation — it's just unreachable. This turns total dead ends into honest, referral-rich starting points for the majority of the potential user base at modest cost.
- **P1 — Make fee waivers and total cost real: link the actual waiver forms (FW-001 / state equivalents), add plain-language eligibility, and show a worst-case total cost line instead of 'varies.'**  
  Most panel personas are low-income; the fee waiver is repeatedly described as the literal difference between affording the process and giving up (P1, P13, P16). 'Varies' actively raises anxiety for exactly the users the tool says it serves.
- **P1 — Confirm X/nonbinary marker availability explicitly per state and per document, and fix Spanish parity + the language-parameter fragility (lang=es vs language=es; toggle must persist through every checklist).**  
  Nonbinary users are core to the product's pitch yet the X marker is never confirmed (P3 blocker), and Spanish — genuinely praised — silently collapses or reverts to English (P2, P8), which for a Spanish-dominant user is a broken promise at the moment of need.
- **P1 — Complete a human screen-reader/keyboard/zoom accessibility pass and publish the results before promoting to assistive-tech users.**  
  Markup is strong but the audit openly lists VoiceOver/NVDA as unchecked (P11). Asking a blind user to be the untested guinea pig on a legal filing is both a trust hit and a liability the team can close with one audit.
- **P2 — Add a plain-language / literacy tier: simplify legalese, add a read-aloud option and icons, surface a visible 'Copied!' confirmation with paste instructions, and add the gating court-order form link/helper.**  
  The user base spans wide literacy and digital-literacy levels; P16 and P8 freeze on legalese and silent UI. These are polish-level fixes that meaningfully widen who can actually complete the flow.
- **P2 — Honor or honestly disable collected-but-ignored inputs (Financial & other records checkbox), and add lightweight handling for common life-context branches: divorce/former-name restoration, interstate move/recognition, and minor/parental-consent.**  
  Silently dropping a selected goal (P18) erodes trust in everything else ('what else is it quietly leaving out?'), and divorce/move/minor are common, well-defined scenarios (P17, P18, P9) that a content addition — not full case logic — can cover.

## Full per-persona findings

### Trans woman in Oakland, CA. First time, overwhelmed but excited. Low savings (needs a fee waiver). Phone-only. Wants name + gender marker on every document.
- **Goal:** partial · **Satisfaction:** 4/5
- **Worked:** • California is one of the five covered states, so I actually got a real, ordered checklist for BOTH name and gender marker - this is the first tool that didn't make me feel invisible.  • The court order step (Step 1) directly told me 'a fee waiver is available if you cannot afford it (Form FW-001)' and that I can request gender recognition at the same time as my name change. That was the single most reassuring thing I read all day.  • It said clearly that NO physician's statement is required and that male, female, OR nonbinary is available - I almost cried, I'd been scared I'd need a doctor's letter.  • Privacy messaging is strong and specific: 'Your answers stay in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to or stored on a server.' As a trans person being careful about my data, that earns trust.  • Every step has a cost, timeline, an official source link with a 'last checked' date (2026-05-31 / 2026-06-16 - recent!), so it feels honest and current.  • The honest banner 'Information, not legal advice. AI-assisted, grounded in cited sources' is on every page - I trust a tool more when it admits what it is.  • The NC-100 form page links the real official form at courts.ca.gov, has a 'copy your details' helper so I type my name once, and says plainly it doesn't fill it in for me. On my phone, copy-paste is exactly how I'd do it anyway.
- **Frictions:**
    - [major] The fee waiver is the whole reason I can afford this, but it's only a one-line mention - no FW-001 link anywhere (not on the checklist, not on the NC-100 form page, not in the detailed answer), and no explanation of how a low-income person qualifies. I had to be told to 'consult the actual form or contact the court.' I'm broke and on a phone; that dead end is exactly where I'm most likely to give up. _(Step 1 (court order) / NC-100 form page / detailed answer page)_
    - [major] Two of my four steps - Social Security (Step 2) and U.S. passport (Step 4) - came back 'Needs reverification, so we don't show it as current' for gender marker.
