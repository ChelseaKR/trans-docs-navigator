emissions station. late fee waiver: you can avoid late fees if the vehicle was not driven on public streets. you must renew via mydmv or in person and complete an affidavit of non-operation. note: for travel within nevada only. “drive-away” permit: if you are leaving the state immediately, you can purchase a special permit at a dmv office that allows you to drive the vehicle to your new destination. former residents: once you have moved and registered your vehicle in your new state, you should surrender your nevada plates and cancel your nevada registration online to avoid any insurance fines or issues. note: if your registration is currently active, you can simply drive to your new state and register there. you do not need a permit unless your nevada registration has payment process. exceptions: you cannot renew online or at a kiosk if the vehicle is registered to a trust or if you are using an exemption transferred from a spouse. these must be done in person. issues: if your voucher number is rejected, contact your county assessor . the dmv cannot fix assessor records. note: apply your exemption during renewal. the dmv does not issue refunds for forgotten exemptions after you have paid. see registration renewal options . i didn't receive a renewal notice. what do i do? it is your responsibility to renew on time. registrations expire on a specific date , not at the end of the month, and late fees are not refunded. check status: use the registration status inquiry requires plate # and last 4 of vin to check your expiration date and insurance status. how to renew: you don't need a notice. get a smog check, if needed, and renew at a kiosk or via mydmv using your plate and vin. address issues: if you moved, the dmv does not use postal forwarding. use a kiosk to get your decal instantly, then file a change of address online. warning: if you renew online with an old address, your decal will be mailed to the wrong house. timing: notices are mailed 30 days before expiration. if your date is further away, it hasn't been sent yet. note: if your registration is suspended due to insurance, you must reinstate it before you can renew. use mydmv to handle both at once. why isn't my online renewal working? if the website isn't processing your renewal, check these common roadblocks: smog check: if required, it must be completed before you try insurance suspensions: check your status via registration inquiry unpaid parking tickets: these must be settled with the specific city or county first. bad debt: any outstanding fees owed to the dmv. technical issues: ensure javascript and cookies are enabled. if the site still fails, try a different browser or use a dmv now kiosk for an instant decal. note: if you are still getting an error moved. what do i do? if you receive a renewal or insurance notice for a vehicle you no longer have: respond to insurance notices: use the insurance verification response portal to report that the vehicle was sold or moved. do not ignore these, as they can trigger suspensions. cancel your registration: log into mydmv to cancel the registration. surrender the plates: if you have them: mail or see former residents . how do i get a vin inspection? you have three main options to get an out-of-state vehicle inspected: dmv office (free): drive to any full-service dmv during business hours. larger offices have a dedicated “vin station” lane on the side or rear of the building. no appointment is needed for the inspection itself. authorized businesses: private companies like smog busters can perform inspections for a small fee. this is often faster than the dmv and doesn't require an appointment. law enforcement: you can have a sworn officer complete the vp 015 certificate . note that many agencies only offer this for residents in rural areas or by special request. what to bring: the vehicle and its out-of-state title or registration. why do you require odometer readings? the nevada legislature directed the dmv to collect odometer readings to collect mileage data to help legislators plan for future road construction funding. see odometer for more details. collapse all can i get a refund of my registration fees? in most cases, no. nevada typically issues a credit rather than a refund. this credit is applied to the registration of your next vehicle. see plate surrender/refunds . note: no refunds are issued to former residents. how can i find out how much my registration fees will be? you can get an estimate or see your exact total using these dmv tools: for a new vehicle: use the online fee estimate . you’ll need the vin and the original msrp, the price when the car was brand new. for a renewal: log into your one-time renewal or mydmv account. it will show your total, including any late fees. note: you must complete a smog check first if one is required. note: nevada fees are based on the original msrp, not what you paid for the car. the tax amount drops by about 10% each year until the vehicle is 9 years old. how are registration fees calculated? your total is based on two main parts: flat fee: $33 for most cars and motorcycles. taxes (gst): calculated using 35% of the original msrp, which then depreciates, drops, as the vehicle ages. local tax: residents in clark or washoe counties pay an extra 1% for road projects. key rule: the tax is always based on the original sticker price, not what you paid for the car used. use the online fee estimate with your vin for an exact total. collapse all i just bought a vehicle. what are the registration requirements? you must register within 30 days of purchase. online: for vehicle purchased from an out-of-state dealer, new resident, private party or gifted. see rapid registration . nevada dealer: if you purchased a vehicle from a nevada dealer, you may be able to register it online without visiting a dmv office. you can transfer existing plates or obtain new plates by mail. they should be providing you with an edrs #. if not, please ask your dealership that you purchased your car from. note: you must have insurance and a movement permit or dealer placard to drive any vehicle on public streets. see vehicle registration requirements . i want to sell a vehicle to a family member or give a vehicle as a gift. transferring a car to a family member is treated as a private party sale. the process: sign the title over to the family member. they can then register it online via rapid registration (drivenv). fees: credits and fees cannot be transferred. the new owner must pay for a full year of registration. plates: normally, you keep your plates for credit, and they get new ones. to let them keep your physical plates, you must sign a license plate release (sp 67) . taxes: gifts and sales to immediate family are exempt from sales tax. insurance: the new owner must get their own nevada insurance before registering. note: even if you “sell” the car for $1, the dmv still calculates registration fees based on the vehicle’s original msrp value. i want to donate a vehicle to charity. donating is treated like a private party sale. follow these steps: the title: you must provide a clear, signed-off title. any loans or liens must be paid off first. remove your plates: always take your plates off. if it protects you from liability, tolls, tickets. it allows you to keep your registration credit for your next car. notify the dmv: complete the vehicle resale notification online via mydmv within 5 days to report the transfer. deadlines: you have 60 days, 30 for specialty plates, to either use the plates on another car or surrender them. receipts: keep all documents from the charity for your tax records. insurance: do not cancel your nevada insurance until after you have removed the plates and notified the dmv. canceling early can trigger automatic fines. i recently paid off a loan or other lien or bought out a lease. what do i need to do? loan payoffs: electronic titles: the bank notifies the dmv, and a clear title is mailed to you automatically. paper titles: the bank signs the “release of lien” and mails it to you. you should then visit the dmv to apply for a fresh “clear” title. lease buyouts: treated as a vehicle sale. you must pay sales tax on the buyout price. update your registration at the dmv to show you as the owner, approx. $5 fee. the dealer/leasing company usually handles the title transfer to you. important: ensure your lender has your correct mailing address before your final payment so your title doesn’t get lost in the mail.
