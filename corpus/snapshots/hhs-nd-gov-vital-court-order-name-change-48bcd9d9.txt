records court order name change court order name change how to update a birth record after a court-ordered name change if you or your child was born in north dakota and you are wanting your name updated on the birth record following a name change, you will need to mail our office the following: certified copy of the court order changing you or your child's name, which must include the current name recorded on the birth record, the new name and the date of birth. certified copy of the court's confidential information form, if current name recorded on birth record, the new name and the date of birth are not included in the court order. completed and notarized birth record amendment application sfn 60183 (2-2026 (pdf) . the $15 amendment fee, made payable to the: nd dhhs. (the amendment fee does not include a certified copy of the updated birth record.) mail all items to: vital records 600 e. boulevard ave., dept. 325 bismarck, nd 58505-0250 if you would like an updated certified copy of your birth record following the name change, you will also need to submit a request for a certified copy of a birth record (sfn 8140 (pdf) , along with an additional $15 per copy requested. do i need to update my birth record after marriage? no. if your name changes as a result of marriage, you should not update your birth record. a certified copy of your marriage record along with your birth record should explain to any agency how your name was changed. footer copyright 2026 north dakota department of health and human services 600 east boulevard ave bismarck, nd 58505-0250 phone: (701) 328-2310 tty users may use relay north dakota at 711 or 1-800-366-6888 accessibility client rights and appeals disclaimer hipaa privacy nondiscrimination contact privacy policy security policy subscribe to news and alerts this website is supported by grants from the administration for children and families (acf), a division for the u.s. department of health and human services. information shared on this site is the responsibility of north dakota health and human services and does not necessarily reflect the views of acf.
